### Memory-pressure incident

The WSL crash cannot be attributed to one proven classical leak. The audit did
directly observe and repair three pressure mechanisms:

1. `npm run test:node` launched effectively unbounded isolated Node/tsx workers,
   many consuming 100–450 MiB. The runner now inserts
   `--test-concurrency=2` by default; `CSTAR_NODE_TEST_CONCURRENCY` is bounded to
   1–8 and invalid values fail closed.
2. PennyOne previously retained repository-wide source and repeated semantic
   processing. Status is now read-only, scanning is explicit, processing is
   one file at a time, model semantics are removed, and hard caps are 2,000
   files, 1 MiB per file, and 32 MiB aggregate.
3. The old session engraver could detach one learning process per session.
   Detached lesson harvesting and model-memory writes are retired. TUI
   blackboard compaction is deterministic with an in-flight heartbeat guard.

The final single-concurrency Node run completed without runaway test workers.
The last post-validation snapshot was approximately 2.6 GiB used, 16 GiB
available, and 96 KiB swap used on a 19 GiB WSL allocation.

The activation window captured the process boundary twice. Before the final
reload, the app server owned three CStar MCP sessions; after reload, all six

one CStar session. The Hermes-owned compatibility bridge remained untouched.
This proves restart cleanup, not a general host-retention guarantee. Continued
multi-session accumulation would still be a host-retention risk rather than
automatic proof of a CStar child leak.
